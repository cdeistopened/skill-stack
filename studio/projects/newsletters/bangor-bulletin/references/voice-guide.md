# Voice Guide: Pirate Wires Meets Tractor Supply

## Core Principles

1. **Smart Brevity** — No fluff. Bold headers, bullet points. Respect the reader's time.
2. **Pro-Neighbor, Pro-Autonomy** — Celebrate self-reliance, local trade, practical cooperation.
3. **Tactical Transparency** — Call it like you see it. If something's good, say so. If something's dumb, say so.
4. **Conversational Authenticity** — Write like you're talking to a neighbor at the feed store.

## Voice Markers

**Use:**
- "Life out here" / "Here's the thing" / "This is how it works"
- Direct address ("you")
- Specific numbers and names
- Contractions
- Admitting what you don't know

**Avoid:**
- Corporate speak ("leverage," "synergy," "ecosystem")
- Bureaucratic language ("pursuant to," "stakeholders")
- Excessive exclamation marks
- Vague language ("soon," "affordable," "various")

## Signature Moves

### The "This Is Insane" Move

When something is obviously absurd, say so plainly:

> "You want to know if anyone's got firewood? Drive to the feed store. Check the board. Squint at a dozen hand-written index cards, half of them six months old. This is insane."

### The "Used to Be Normal" Move

Frame cooperative practices as a return to common sense:

> "This is the kind of thing that used to be normal. Your neighbor had a bull, you borrowed him. Somewhere along the way we forgot that proximity is an asset, not an inconvenience."

### The "Let's Be Honest" Move

Cut through BS and say what everyone's thinking:

> "Hall maintenance isn't cheap. This is how the community keeps the lights on."

## Words That Kill Newsletters

- "In today's edition..."
- "This week we'll explore..."
- "Without further ado..."
- "It goes without saying..."

Jump straight into content instead.
