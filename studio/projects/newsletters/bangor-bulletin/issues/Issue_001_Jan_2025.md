## The Lead: Why You're Reading This

Here's the thing about living out here: information travels at the speed of a truck.

You want to know if anyone's got firewood? Drive to the feed store. Check the board. Squint at a dozen hand-written index cards, half of them six months old. Want to know hay prices? Same deal. Lost dog? Hope you drove by the post office today.

This is insane. We've got phones in our pockets that can summon a car in San Francisco, but in Bangor you still have to physically show up somewhere to find out if Greg's got oak.

The Bangor Bulletin is the fix. Once a week, you get the board — digitized, organized, and sent to your phone. Events, services, goods, lost pets. Everything that matters, nothing that doesn't. Five minutes, and you're caught up.

But here's where it gets interesting.

**The Boar Problem**

Right now, there's a Duroc boar named Han Solo making the rounds in Butte County. Han belongs to Matt Eakin — contractor, pig farmer, future sponsor of this newsletter (we're working on it). Matt doesn't need Han year-round. A boar's only "working" a few weeks out of the year. The rest of the time? He's eating. Loudly. And getting on his lady friends' nerves.

So instead of every small farm maintaining its own boar — expensive, inefficient, kind of ridiculous — we rotate Han through a network. You sign up for a window. Han shows up. Does his job. You feed him while he's there. When your sows are bred, you coordinate with the next person in line.

No fees. No contracts. Just a trust-based arrangement among people who'd rather share resources than duplicate them.

This is the kind of thing that used to be normal. Your neighbor had a bull, you borrowed him. Someone had a hay baler, everyone used it. Somewhere along the way we forgot that proximity is an asset, not an inconvenience.

**What This Newsletter Is For**

The Bulletin is the connective tissue. It's how you find out Greg's got oak before you waste a trip. It's how you learn that Sara Specht does emergency livestock calls at 2am. It's how the boar share finds new members.

More trust. More connections. Less driving to check a bulletin board.

Welcome to Issue #1. Let's see what we can build.

## Featured: Borrow a Boar

**Looking for a few more farms to join our boar-sharing circle.**

The pitch is simple: keeping a boar year-round to breed a handful of sows is expensive. Feed costs add up, and he's only working a few weeks out of the year. So instead of everyone maintaining their own boar, we rotate one through a network of small farms.

**How it works:**

- Sign up for a window when you need him (8 weeks to 5 months)
- He shows up, does his job, you feed him while he's there
- When your sows are bred, coordinate with the next person in line
- No fees, no contracts — just trust

**What we're looking for:**

- Small-scale pig farmers who need occasional breeding access
- Secure fencing and basic pig sense
- Good neighbors who communicate
- New members need a vouch from someone already in the circle

**Current coverage:** Butte County and surrounding. Open to expanding if logistics work.

If you're raising a few pigs and tired of paying for AI or hauling sows somewhere for breeding, this might be a fit.
