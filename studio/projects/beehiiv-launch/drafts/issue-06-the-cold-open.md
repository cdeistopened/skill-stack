# Issue 6: The Cold Open

*Why your intros suck.*

---

"Welcome back to the show. Today we have an amazing guest. His name is..."

You've already stopped listening. So has everyone else. Podcast intros kill retention.

The first 30 seconds determine whether someone keeps listening. And most podcasters waste them on pleasantries.

---

Here's what works instead: the cold open.

No intro. No welcome. No setup. Just drop the listener into the middle of something compelling.

"I was $400,000 in debt when I realized the whole system was rigged."

Now you're listening.

---

The trick is that cold opens don't come from nowhere.

You don't write them. You cut them. From the conversation you already recorded.

Somewhere in your 45-minute episode, your guest said something provocative, vulnerable, or surprising. You find that moment, pull it to the front, and let it roll for 20 seconds before cutting to your intro.

The intro still exists. It just comes after the hook.

---

Why does this work?

Psychology. Humans are wired to complete open loops.

When someone says "I was $400,000 in debt..." your brain can't help but wonder: How? What happened? Are they okay now?

That question keeps you listening through the intro. Through the background. Through the parts that would normally make you skip ahead.

The cold open earns you time.

---

Here's the format I use:

**0:00 - 0:25:** Cold open clip. Drops listener into something interesting.

**0:25 - 0:30:** Brief cut. Usually music or a sound cue.

**0:30 - 1:00:** Standard intro. Now you do the welcome, the guest background, the episode preview.

**1:00+:** Episode begins.

Total investment: finding and cutting one clip. Maybe 10 minutes of work. The ROI is massive.

---

How do you find the right clip?

I scan the transcript. Look for:

- Moments of vulnerability ("I didn't tell anyone for years...")
- Surprising claims ("Everyone thinks X, but actually...")
- High stakes ("If I didn't figure this out...")
- Conflict or tension ("We almost didn't make it...")

Usually there's one or two clear winners. The moment you read it and think "wait, what?" - that's your cold open.

---

A few rules:

**Keep it short.** 20-35 seconds max. You're creating a hook, not a trailer.

**Use verbatim dialogue.** Don't paraphrase or summarize. Let the speaker's actual words do the work.

**Don't resolve the tension.** Cut before the explanation. The listener should have a question in their mind, not an answer.

**Match the tone.** If your episode is playful, the cold open should be playful. If it's heavy, the cold open can be heavy. Just don't mismatch.

---

This applies beyond podcasts.

YouTube videos? Cold open. Cut to you saying something interesting before the logo animation.

Blog posts? Cold open. Start with a scene or claim, not "In this article I will discuss..."

Newsletters? You're reading one. I didn't start with "Welcome to issue 6."

The principle is universal: earn attention before you ask for it.

---

Most content starts with throat-clearing.

Background. Context. Setup. Qualifications. All the stuff you think people need before they can understand your point.

They don't. They need a reason to care. Give them that first. The context can come after.
