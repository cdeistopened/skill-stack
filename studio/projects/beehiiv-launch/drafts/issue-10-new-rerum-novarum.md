# Issue 10: A New Rerum Novarum

*AI and the future of work.*

---

In 1891, Pope Leo XIII looked at the industrial revolution and saw a fork in the road.

The new machines had created unprecedented wealth. They had also created unprecedented misery. Capital was concentrating. Workers were ground down. The middle path between capitalism and socialism was disappearing.

His response was *Rerum Novarum* - "Of New Things." A document arguing that technology should serve human dignity, not the other way around.

We're at the same fork now.

---

Here's the thing about AI that everyone's missing:

The technology itself is neutral. What matters is the structure we build around it.

**Path One:** AI concentrates power. Big companies use it to squeeze more productivity from fewer workers. Creative work gets commodified. The gap widens. Gilded Age 2.0.

**Path Two:** AI distributes power. Solo creators compete with conglomerates. The local newsletter rivals institutional media. The freelancer has the leverage that used to require a team.

Same technology. Opposite outcomes. The question is which path we choose.

---

I'm not being abstract here.

This is playing out right now. In real companies. In real careers.

I watched it happen at my day job. What used to require five people now requires me and a skill stack. Not because AI replaced those roles. Because AI gave me access to capabilities I didn't have before.

The company got more productive. I got more valuable. The dynamic worked.

But I also see the other version. Workers asked to produce more with AI assistance while wages stay flat. The human becomes the AI supervisor - handling exceptions, reviewing outputs. Doing the worst of both worlds.

---

The difference is ownership.

If you use AI through your employer's systems, for their purposes, with their data - you're a worker. Slightly more productive, but still dependent.

If you build your own skills, your own voice models, your own workflows - you're an owner. The capabilities are yours. They transfer. They compound.

This is the distributist insight: the problem isn't property, it's concentration of property. Distribute the tools widely and the system works for everyone.

---

What does this mean practically?

**Own your tools.** Your skills live in markdown files you control. Your voice model is yours. Your workflows are portable. Not locked into someone else's platform.

**Connect to reality.** Pure digital content is a race to the bottom. Everyone has AI now. The defensible position is where AI meets the physical world - local communities, specialized knowledge, services that require showing up.

**Build skills that compound.** Every skill you create is an asset. Every workflow you document is leverage. The person who invests this time now will be impossible to catch later.

**Refuse to be diminished.** When someone frames your role as "AI manager" or "prompt engineer" or "human in the loop" - reject the framing. You're not supervising the machine. You're using it to amplify what you already know.

---

This newsletter has been about skills.

Technical stuff. Workflows. How to dictate better, match your voice, avoid AI tells, build wizards.

But underneath all of it is a philosophy: liberation, not replacement.

AI can be a tool that frees you to do better work. Or it can be a tool that makes you obsolete. The technology doesn't care. The structure determines the outcome.

---

*Rerum Novarum* argued that workers had dignity independent of their productive output. That they deserved fair wages, the right to organize, the chance to own property.

The same principles apply now.

Your value doesn't come from what you produce. It comes from your judgment, your relationships, your specific knowledge and perspective. AI is the lever, not the source.

---

The people who understand this are building differently.

They're not just learning AI tools. They're building systems that let them own the tools. They're not just producing content. They're connecting that content to communities, services, things that matter.

They're choosing distribution over concentration. Every skill they build, every workflow they document, spreads the capability wider.

This is the bet: in a world where AI commoditizes generic work, the people who build unique systems win.

---

So where does this newsletter go from here?

More skills. More workflows. More tools you can own and modify.

But also: more on the philosophy. Why this matters. What we're building toward.

The technology will keep getting better. The question is whether we'll structure it to serve human flourishing or human diminishment.

I know which path I'm choosing.
