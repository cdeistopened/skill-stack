---

How does this work?

Most people think voice is mysterious. Ineffable. The product of years of experience that can't be reduced to rules.

They're half right.

Voice does emerge from accumulated decisions. But those decisions form patterns. And patterns can be documented.

---

I analyzed my own writing to figure out what makes it sound like me. Here's what I found:

**Vocabulary:** I use short Anglo-Saxon words instead of Latinate ones. "Use" not "utilize." "Start" not "commence." "Help" not "facilitate."

**Rhythm:** I vary sentence length deliberately. Long sentences that build and layer and connect and spiral. Then short ones. For punch.

**Structure:** I use fragments. Constantly. Not grammatically correct. But rhythmically right.

**Perspective:** First person, specific stories, admission of mistakes. I don't claim authority I don't have.

**What I never do:** Correlative constructions ("X isn't just Y - it's Z"). Hedge words when I'm certain. Corporate jargon. Throat-clearing.

That's it. Five categories. Maybe twenty specific observations. Enough to teach an AI to write like me.

---

The trick is examples.

Rules alone don't work. You can tell Claude "write in a conversational tone" a thousand times. It won't help. The phrase is too vague. Claude's idea of conversational is different from yours.

But show Claude three examples of your conversational tone? Now it has something concrete to match.

The skill format I use:

1. **Voice characteristics** - The patterns described in plain language
2. **Do this / don't do this** - Specific rules
3. **Examples** - Real samples from your writing, annotated

The examples do the heavy lifting.

---

Here's the counterintuitive part: five words is often enough.

Not five rules. Five *words*.

When I analyzed writers I admire, I found they each have a handful of signature moves. Phrases they use constantly. Structural tics. Vocabulary choices.

Packy McCormick starts paragraphs with "So." Naval ends sentences with "that's it." Paul Graham says "the thing is" before making a point.

These tiny patterns, documented, teach Claude more than pages of abstract description.

---

Why does this matter?

Because the #1 complaint about AI writing is that it all sounds the same.

Generic. Corporate. Smooth in a way that feels slippery. The verbal equivalent of stock photos.

Voice skills fix this. They give Claude a specific target to match. The output stops sounding like AI and starts sounding like a person.

Which is the whole point.

---

A few things people get wrong about voice:

**"My writing isn't consistent enough to have a voice."** Yes it is. Your inconsistencies are part of your voice. The places where you break rules, the topics that make you tangent, the sentences you'd never write - all signal.

**"Voice is too personal to document."** Voice is *patterns*. Patterns can be documented. The documentation isn't the voice. It's a map that helps Claude navigate toward it.

**"This will make everyone sound the same."** The opposite. It will make you sound more like yourself. The default AI voice is what everyone sounds the same. Voice skills break the default.
