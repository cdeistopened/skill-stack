This document outlines the strategic architecture for Skill Stack, a multimedia brand focused on demonstrating high-level AI workflows through practical use cases.
1. The Core Philosophy: Skill Stacking
The project is built on the 4 S Structure (a consistent creative process applied to any output). The goal is to show how "lower-skilled" individuals can emulate professional outputs by using a "stack" of AI-assisted skills.
 * Practicality: Content is driven by real-world use cases (e.g., "I created a local newsletter") rather than abstract theory.
 * The Skill File: Skills are treated as modular components. They require a balance of universal applicability and user-specific personalization.
 * Iterative Learning: The YouTube channel serves as a "progressive evolution" of content, acknowledging that static courses become outdated too quickly.
2. The Project Ecosystem
The architecture consists of three main pillars:
A. Media & Content (The Top of Funnel)
 * YouTube Channel: The primary discovery engine. It features "live-vibe" builds and tutorials where the creator executes a project (e.g., creating a local newsletter) in real-time.
 * Newsletter: A companion to the channel for deeper dives and community engagement.
 * "The Bangor Wire": A flagship pilot project. A hyper-local newsletter generated by taking photos of community bulletin boards and post offices, using OCR (Optical Character Recognition) via Gemini to ingest data, and spinning up a website to host the feed.
B. The Utility Stack (The "Doodle Reader" Pipeline)
A proprietary workflow designed to turn raw information into actionable "Skills":
 * Ingestion: Use Doodle Reader to convert YouTube URLs or podcasts into clean Markdown transcripts.
 * Synthesis: Feed transcripts into an AI (like Claude) with a Skill Creator prompt.
 * Output: Generate a specific "Skill" based on the proprietary information found in the transcripts.
C. Product & Monetization
 * Free Layer: GitHub repositories or ZIP files containing "Skill Starter Packs" to encourage email sign-ups.
 * Premium Layer: A potential subscription for Doodle Reader (Premium features: podcast/video transcription, universal Markdown).
 * Consulting/Franchising: Proposing a "Local Newsletter-in-a-Box" model where others can partner with local businesses (anchor sponsors) to replicate "The Wire" model in their own towns.
3. Implementation Strategy
 * Low Friction Entry: Start with simple, focused builds (e.g., just using bulletin board photos) before moving to complex news-scraping automation.
 * Community Feedback Loop: Use early subscriber interactions to identify friction points (e.g., "I can't install Cursor") to inform future "Skill" tutorials.
 * Vibe Coding vs. Deterministic Apps: Acknowledge the tension between AI-driven "skills" (which require human-in-the-loop oversight) and "vibe coding" (where AI builds standalone, deterministic applications).

### progressive disclosure for content databases

A central problem is progressive disclosure for content databases.

When writing a book chapter based on a large body of content, you need a solution like RAG that can search your sources and surface relevant parts for a given task without overloading the context window.

The ideal workflow: take your overarching task, split off a sub-task that uses its entire context window to draft that section with key details, then feed it back.

You also need a system like a Notion AI database that constantly updates with summaries, code maps, and custom details—essentially YAML front matter. This could integrate with Obsidian bases so that whenever you add a new source, it enters the content database. Those pieces of content don't surface until they're needed.

Currently, Notebook LM and Notion AI are probably the best at this overall. They can look through your whole database and identify what's important. But they're not really containerized or as useful because you can't inject them into a conversation with Claude in quite the same way—or can you?
