# Checkpoint 1: Story Discovery - Lewis Kallow Episode

## Primary Story Arc: "The 2000-Word Prompt Problem"

**Protagonist:** Lewis Kallow (AI productivity writer)

| Beat | Content | Timestamp |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **Setup** | Lewis writes detailed AI prompts - 2000+ words with meticulous instructions | ~00:01:00 |
| **Disaster** | Results aren't better than simple prompts. All that effort, wasted. | ~00:02:30 |
| **Failed Approach** | Adding MORE detail to prompts. If 2000 words doesn't work, try 3000. | ~00:03:00 |
| **Insight** | "A few months after that article came out, there was a study, an academic study that was released and it looked at over 500 prompting techniques. And it found that the single most effective prompting technique is just showing the AI examples." | 00:03:46-00:04:25 |
| **Resolution** | Lewis shifts from instruction-heavy prompts to example-based prompts | ~00:05:00 |
| **Reflection** | The counterintuitive truth: less instruction, more demonstration. "Examples beat instructions." | ~00:06:00 |

**The Big Idea:**
Most people write AI prompts backwards - they pile on instructions when they should be showing examples. The academic research is clear: across 500 techniques studied, examples beat everything else.

**Strength Assessment:**
- [x] Complete arc (6 beats present)
- [x] Emotional stakes clear (Lewis's wasted effort)
- [x] Insight is non-obvious (more detail ≠ better results)
- [x] Reflection has universal application (everyone writes prompts)

## Selected Arc: **A - "The 2000-Word Prompt Problem"**

**Why this arc wins:**
1. **Universality** - Everyone who uses AI writes prompts; this applies to all of them
2. **Counterintuitive insight** - "More detail = worse results" surprises people
3. **Research backing** - The academic study gives it credibility beyond opinion
4. **Actionable** - Viewer can immediately try showing examples instead of instructing
5. **Clean structure** - All 6 beats are clearly present and quotable
