Your AI prompts are probably too long. Lewis Kallow - ghostwriter, Every.to contributor, and co-author of The Action Digest with Scott Belsky - discovered that 5 examples beat 2,000 words of instructions. Every time.

In this episode, we go deep on the examples-over-instructions method for AI prompting. Lewis shares the "narrative snippets" technique he uses to extract stories from source material - a 6-beat structure that turns transcripts into compelling content.

We also live-demo building this into a Claude Code skill, talk about the "cheating on work" phenomenon sweeping knowledge workers, and explore why subtraction beats addition when designing AI workflows.

## Editing Patterns to Apply

### Patterns to Remove Throughout:
- "Uh," "um," "you know," "like"
- Repeated words ("And, and, and")
- Trailing phrases that don't add meaning
- "I think" when it weakens the statement
- Filler at start of sentences ("And so," "So," "Yeah, and")

### Patterns to Keep:
- Natural repetition for emphasis ("everyone, everyone")
- Honest hesitations that feel real (occasional "kind of")
- The energy and enthusiasm in delivery
- Back-and-forth momentum between hosts
