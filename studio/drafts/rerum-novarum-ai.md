# A New Rerum Novarum: AI as a Tool of Liberation

*Draft - Lead Essay*

---

Pope Leo XIII wrote *Rerum Novarum* in 1891 to address the condition of workers in the industrial age. The new machines had created unprecedented wealth - and unprecedented misery. Capital accumulated. Labor was ground down. The Church's response: a third way between unchecked capitalism and socialist revolution. Dignified work. Just wages. The rights of workers to organize. Property widely distributed.

We need a new *Rerum Novarum* for the age of artificial intelligence.

The new pope seems to sense this. The question is whether we'll get it right this time.

---

## The Fork in the Road

Here's the theological reality that the AI doomers and the AI utopians both miss: there is so much work to be done that we should not feel threatened by machines taking our jobs.

Look around. The work is everywhere:

- Local communities falling apart for lack of connective tissue
- Small businesses drowning in administrative overhead
- Knowledge trapped in the heads of retiring craftsmen
- Children without mentors
- Elderly without visitors
- Land without stewards
- Churches without teachers
- Towns without newspapers

The work is infinite. The question is: who will do it, and under what conditions?

AI creates a fork in the road. Two paths diverge:

**Path One: AI as a tool of concentration**

Big business uses AI to squeeze more productivity from fewer workers. The remaining jobs become more menial - the tasks AI can't quite crack. Middle management disappears. Creative work gets commoditized. The gap between owners and workers widens. We get a new Gilded Age, but shinier.

**Path Two: AI as a tool of distribution**

Small operators use AI to do what only large organizations could do before. The local newsletter competes with the media conglomerate. The solo consultant delivers what the consulting firm delivered. The craftsman documents and shares knowledge that would have died with him. The entrepreneur, the creator, the small business owner - they get leverage that was previously reserved for those with capital to hire staff.

Same technology. Radically different outcomes.

---

## The Distributist Case for AI

G.K. Chesterton and Hilaire Belloc argued that the problem wasn't property, but the concentration of property. The solution wasn't collectivism (which just concentrates property in the state) but *distribution* - spreading ownership as widely as possible. Three acres and a cow. The shopkeeper, the smallholder, the craftsman.

They lost that argument in the twentieth century. Scale economies won. You couldn't compete with the factory, the chain store, the conglomerate. The small operator was squeezed out not by force but by efficiency.

AI changes the math.

The cost of producing content approaches zero. The cost of managing complexity drops. The cost of coordinating work falls. The advantages of scale erode.

A single person with the right skills can now:
- Publish a newsletter that rivals institutional media
- Produce a podcast with professional quality
- Run customer service that feels personal
- Manage projects that used to require teams
- Create marketing that competes with agencies

This is the distributist moment - if we seize it.

## The Condition of the Digital Worker

But there's a dark version of this future too.

Look at what's already happening. The gig economy promised freedom and delivered precarity. The creator economy promised independence and delivered burnout. Remote work promised flexibility and delivered the dissolution of boundaries between work and life.

The AI economy could go the same way:

- Workers asked to produce more with AI assistance, but wages don't rise
- Creative work devalued because "AI can do that"
- The human becomes the supervisor of the machine, handling edge cases and exceptions - the worst of both worlds
- Constant upskilling treadmill as the baseline keeps rising
- Surveillance increases as AI makes monitoring trivially cheap

*Rerum Novarum* addressed the condition of factory workers. We need to address the condition of digital workers - and the AI-assisted worker in particular.

---

## Liberation, Not Replacement

The framing matters enormously.

Wrong framing: "AI will take your job."
Right framing: "AI can help you do work that matters."

Wrong framing: "Learn AI or become obsolete."
Right framing: "AI can amplify what you already know how to do."

Wrong framing: "AI generates content."
Right framing: "AI transforms your ideas into polished form."

The difference isn't semantic. It's theological.

Work has dignity because humans have dignity. The value of work comes from the person doing it, not from the output produced. When we frame AI as replacement, we reduce humans to their productive output - and find them wanting. When we frame AI as amplification, we start from human capacity and ask how to extend it.

Leo XIII: "It is shameful and inhuman to treat men like chattels to make money by, or to look upon them merely as so much muscle or physical power."

The same principle applies to mental work. It is shameful to treat knowledge workers as interchangeable prompt-runners, or to look upon them merely as so much cognitive processing power.

## The Practical Path

So what do we actually do?

**1. Own your tools**

The difference between liberation and subjugation is often just: who owns the means of production? 

If you use AI through your employer's systems, for your employer's purposes, you're a worker. If you use AI through your own systems, for your own purposes, you're an owner.

This means: your own workflows, your own voice models, your own knowledge base, your own client relationships. Portable. Yours.

**2. Connect to the real world**

Pure digital content is a red ocean. Everyone has access to the same AI. Competing on content alone is a race to the bottom.

The defensible position is where AI meets physical reality:
- Local communities that can't be served by global platforms
- Specialized knowledge that requires real experience
- Services that require showing up
- Relationships that require trust built over time

Start a local newsletter. Serve your neighbors. Document expertise that comes from doing the work. The content supports the real thing - it doesn't replace it.

**3. Build skills, not just outputs**

The person who learns to use AI effectively is building capital. The person who just uses AI to produce faster is running on a treadmill.

Skills compound. Outputs are consumed.

Learn how to capture your voice. Learn how to structure your knowledge. Learn how to build workflows that get better over time. These are assets that appreciate.

**4. Refuse to be diminished**

There will be pressure to accept less. "AI can do most of that, so we'll pay you less." "AI handles the creative work, you just check it." "Your role is to manage the AI."

Refuse the frame. 

You are not a prompt engineer. You are not an AI supervisor. You are not a content reviewer.

You are someone with knowledge, judgment, and relationships. AI helps you apply those at scale. The value is in you - AI is just the lever.

---

## The Work That Remains

Leo XIII saw that the industrial revolution had created a choice. The wealth could concentrate in fewer and fewer hands, or it could be distributed widely through just structures and worker organization.

We face the same choice.

AI is going to transform work. The question is whether it transforms work in ways that serve human dignity, or in ways that further reduce humans to economic inputs.

The work that remains - the work that AI enables rather than replaces - is enormous:

- Rebuilding local institutions
- Capturing and transmitting knowledge
- Creating connection in isolated communities
- Serving people who are currently underserved
- Making small-scale enterprise viable again

This is not a moment for fear. It's a moment for building.

But it requires intention. It requires choosing the path of distribution over concentration. It requires refusing to be diminished. It requires building skills and owning tools.

The work is infinite. The tools are available. The question is whether we'll seize them.

---

*Don't get stuck as a worker in the future.*

*Own the tools. Serve real communities. Build skills that compound.*

*AI can be a tool of liberation - if we choose to use it that way.*
