# Skill Stack YouTube Strategy

## The Thesis

Today, a single well-equipped writer can do the work of an entire production team. You just need a clean workspace for frictionless content engineering.

**The one-person mini-media empire.** That's the frame.

---

## Target Audience

### Primary: The "Noah Ryan" Type

- 50K-200K followers on X/LinkedIn
- Established voice/expertise in their niche
- Producing content manually - it's slow, painful
- Considered hiring a ghostwriter ($3-5K/month)
- Not technical, but tech-curious
- Values authenticity (doesn't want generic AI slop)

**Their pain:**
- "I know what I want to say but writing takes forever"
- "I have years of content but it's scattered across platforms"
- "Ghostwriters don't sound like me"
- "I don't have time to learn to code"

**The pitch:**
Instead of paying a ghostwriter $60K/year, spend 10 hours learning skills. Then you have:
- Your voice, captured and replicable
- Your archive, as a searchable database
- Your style, as a skill
- 5x speed, permanently

### Secondary: Local entrepreneurs / small operators

People who want to start something (newsletter, podcast, local business) but think they need a team. Show them they don't.

---

## The "Sourcery" Frame

**S-O-U-R-C-E-ry** - You're wielding your own source material like a wizard.

You're not technical, but you're using these tools to their full capability. The magic isn't generation from nothing - it's transformation of what you already have.

Key insight that's allergic-to-data people need to hear:

> "I've always been allergic to data and process, thinking they interfered with creativity. But now you have an assistant who you can delegate the nitty-gritty to - infinitely willing to get into the details and just tell you what you need to do."

---

## Format

**Target length:** ~10 minutes (optimized for X video)

**Structure:**
- 0:00-0:30 - Hook (weird/concrete story or pain point)
- 0:30-2:00 - The problem + why this skill matters
- 2:00-7:00 - Live demo (one skill, real transformation)
- 7:00-9:00 - The finished skill walkthrough
- 9:00-10:00 - CTA + next episode tease

**Clip-worthy moments to engineer:**
- The "aha" transformation (before/after)
- A quotable one-liner
- The weird story beat
- The skill reveal (show the markdown)

## The Notion → Obsidian Episode

This deserves early placement (Episode 2) because it addresses a core insight:

**Thesis:** All these tools that layer on top of your data are becoming a hindrance in this new era.

**The story:**
- Had years of content in Notion
- Notion's proprietary format made it hard to use with AI tools
- Migrated everything to Obsidian (plain markdown)
- Now everything is text - the most flexible format
- AI tools can read/write directly to your files

**The lesson:**
- Own your data in plain text
- Tools come and go, text is forever
- The simpler the format, the more powerful the AI integration

This sets up why the workspace architecture matters.

## The Build-in-Public Angle

Show the workspace progressively:
- Episode 1: Empty folder → first skill
- Episode 2-4: Add memory protocol, brand docs
- Episode 5-8: Add content production skills
- Episode 9+: Add growth/systems skills

By the end, viewers have seen the whole architecture emerge.

**Tension:** "I work faster when doing vs. presenting"

**Solution:** Record the doing, then do a quick voiceover/edit. Don't try to explain while building in real-time. Capture, then narrate.

## Rerum Novarum Integration

Episode 1 (The Local Newsletter) should open with the theological/philosophical frame:

> "AI creates a fork in the road. Path one: tool of concentration - big business squeezes more from fewer workers. Path two: tool of distribution - small operators do what only large organizations could do before. Same technology, radically different outcomes."

Then the Bangor Bulletin becomes the concrete proof. One person, serving 500 neighbors, using skills to do what would have required a small team.
