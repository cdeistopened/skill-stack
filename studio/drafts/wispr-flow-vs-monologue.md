I've dictated over 900,000 words using Wispr Flow, which puts me somewhere in the top 1% of their users. I had no real complaints - the latency was good, the accuracy excellent, the interface unobtrusive enough that I could forget about it and just talk. Then I switched to Monologue, and I've been trying to figure out how to explain why.

[Monologue](https://every.to/monologue) is Every.to's dictation app. If you're not familiar with Every, they're a writing-focused company that Dan Shipper has been building for years, and his essays on AI-assisted writing helped shape how I think about this whole space. They're serious people who care about craft, which matters when you're trusting a tool with your words. Monologue uses the same underlying technology as everything else - OpenAI's Whisper - but wraps it in a different interface philosophy.

What I like better about Monologue is hard to articulate without sounding precious about it. The app disappears. When you're not actively dictating, it tucks itself to the side of the screen, out of your visual field entirely. Wispr Flow floated in a more prominent position, always visible, a little notification bubble reminding you it was there. Small difference, but dictation is about flow, and anything that catches your eye - even something helpful, even something you put there on purpose - can break that flow in ways you don't notice until they're gone.

The latency feels equal or slightly better than Wispr Flow, though I'll admit the comparison isn't perfectly controlled. What actually prompted the switch was that one particular day Wispr Flow was loading slowly enough that I got frustrated and decided to finally try the alternative I'd been hearing about. Monologue has been consistently snappy since, but whether that's the app or my internet connection or the phase of the moon, I couldn't say for certain.

What Monologue still needs to fix is the way it picks up the beginning of your speech. The first word sometimes gets truncated - the app doesn't quite catch the audio fast enough when I press the button. I have a tendency to start talking immediately, press and speak in one fluid motion, and Monologue occasionally misses the opening syllable. I'll say "The problem is..." and it will transcribe "problem is..." as if I'd started mid-sentence.

This is a small thing. It's also the kind of small thing that compounds over thousands of dictations. If I'm unconsciously starting every sentence with "And" or "So" as a verbal run-up to give the app time to wake up, that's friction I didn't have before, and it shapes the way I think while I'm talking. I expect they'll fix this - Every iterates on their products - but until then I've trained myself to pause a beat after pressing the button, which works fine once you remember to do it.

I'm sticking with Monologue anyway. The interface refinements outweigh the audio pickup issue, and Every's track record suggests they'll keep improving it.

But here's the real point, the reason I'm even writing this: the tool matters less than the practice. Pick a dictation app - Wispr Flow, Monologue, the built-in one on your phone, whatever - and start talking. The refinements come later. The habit comes first. Once you're dictating consistently, once it's just how you capture ideas, you'll know what tradeoffs matter to you and which ones you can live with.

The point isn't to find the perfect tool. The point is to [learn to flow](/learn-to-flow).
